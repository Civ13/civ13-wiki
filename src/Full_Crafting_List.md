# Full Crafting List

The complete recipe list is the **[Crafting Atlas](https://civ13.github.io/Civ13/crafting/)**. It is generated from the game source every time a change is merged, so it always matches the current game: every crafting, anvil, workbench and processing recipe with its sprite, ingredients, tools, research and era requirements.

How to use it:

- **Search** by item name, resource or research node. Searching a resource (for example `clay`) also lists everything that produces or uses it.
- **Filter** by recipe system (hand crafting, anvil, workbench, factory machines), by unlock era, or by the processor that makes it.
- **Open a recipe** to see its exact conditions: research points, map or faction gates, skill and tool requirements, and the source line it was read from.

For how crafting works in play, read the [Crafting guide](guides/Guide_to_Crafting.md); for skill thresholds, [Crafting Skill Requirements](Crafting_Skill_Level_Requirements.md).

## Common materials

The materials most recipes start from, and where the guides explain how to get them. Search the material's name in the atlas for everything it makes.

### Wood

Chop trees with an axe for logs, then saw or craft them into planks. See [Wood](guides/Guide_to_Crafting.md#wood) in the Crafting guide.

### Stone

Mine rock with a pickaxe or gather loose stones. Stone bricks are cut from it for sturdier walls.

### Sandstone

Mined in deserts and dry biomes; used like stone for blocks, statues and obelisks.

### Marble

A rarer stone for statues, columns and decorated floors.

### Obsidian

Volcanic glass for sharp early blades and arrowheads.

### Clay

Pour water on a dirt pile to make clay, then fire it in a kiln or oven. See [Clay](guides/Guide_to_Construction.md#clay) in the Construction guide.

### Glass

Fire sand in a campfire or oven for glass sheets.

### Cloth

Grow cotton or flax and process it on a loom. See the [Loom](guides/Guide_to_Crafting.md#loom) in the Crafting guide.

### Woolcloth

Shear sheep, then weave the wool on a [loom](guides/Guide_to_Crafting.md#loom).

### Rope

Ret hemp in a [retting trough](guides/Guide_to_Crafting.md#retting-trough) and twist the fibres into rope.

### Leather

Butcher animals for pelts and tan them on a [tanning rack](guides/Guide_to_Crafting.md#tanning-rack); see [Leather tanning](Miscellaneous_Crafting.md#leather-tanning).

### Bone

Butcher animals; bones craft into tools, needles and decorations.

## Metals

Smelt ore in a furnace and work the ingots on an anvil. The [Metallurgy guide](guides/Guide_to_Metallurgy.md) covers ores, furnaces, alloys and the anvil.

### Copper

The first workable metal: smelt copper ore for tools, wire and early weapons.

### Tin

Smelt tin ore; alloyed with copper it makes bronze.

### Bronze

Copper and tin alloyed in the furnace; the Bronze Age standard for weapons, armor and tools.

### Zinc

Melt calamine (zinc ore) on its own; used for zinc electroplating and, alloyed with copper, for brass.

### Brass

Copper and calamine alloyed in the kiln, from 313 B.C.; used for ammunition casings, the trombone and handbell, globes, razor blades and statues.

### Iron

Smelt iron ore; stronger than bronze and the base of most medieval and later recipes.

### Steel

Refined from iron in the furnace; the strongest material for hand weapons, armor, machine parts and modern construction.

### Lead

Soft metal for bullets, shot and weights.

### Silver

Precious metal for coins, jewelry and decoration.

### Gold

Precious metal for coins, jewelry and decoration.

### Electronics
