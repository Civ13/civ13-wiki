# Tool Materials

Most tools can be made from more than one material. A softer material is easier to come by and works more slowly; a better one works faster. You are never locked out of a job for lacking iron, you are only slower at it.

## Reading a material

Examine any tool, weapon or part made of a material to see a **Material** line. It describes the material in plain words, such as how firm, tough and hard-wearing it is and whether it burns, rusts or throws sparks.

## Picks, shovels and hatchets

The head sets how fast the tool works. Heads you cast in moulds or assemble from a head and a handle follow the same order as the ready-made tools:

| Head | Pickaxe | Shovel | Hatchet |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bone | Slowest | Slowest | Slowest |
| Flint or stone | Slow | Slow | Slow |
| Copper | Between stone and iron | Between bone and iron | Between stone and bronze |
| Bronze | A little slower than iron | A little slower than iron | A little faster than iron |
| Iron | The usual speed | The usual speed | The usual speed |
| Steel | Fastest | Fastest | Fastest |

A light handle speeds a pick or shovel up a little and a heavy one slows it down. A steel head on a tool you assemble yourself works at the full steel speed.

## Hand tools in other metals

Hold a stack of the metal, open its crafting menu and look in **Tools**. The tool comes out named for its metal and tinted to match.

| Tool | Usual material | Also made from | Research |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Hammer | Wood (5) | Copper, bronze or steel (3) | Basic Toolmaking |
| Chisel | Stone, sandstone or iron | Bone (4), copper, bronze or steel (8) | Basic Toolmaking |
| Wrench | Iron (5) | Bronze or steel | Basic Toolmaking |
| Shears | Bronze or iron (2) | Copper or steel | Basic Agriculture |
| Screwdriver | Iron (2) | Bronze or steel | Iron Tools |
| Saw | Iron (3) | Copper, bronze or steel | Iron Tools |
| Crowbar | Iron (4) | Bronze or steel | Iron Tools |
| Wirecutters | Steel (4) | Iron or bronze | Iron Tools |

Compared with the usual tool, copper works at about two thirds of the speed, bronze a little under full speed and steel a little over. You feel it when you saw logs into planks and when you fasten or unfasten something with a wrench.

## Recipes that need a second material

Some builds need a second material as well as the one you are holding. A raft needs 2 rope and a carriage needs 20. The second material can be in your other hand, worn on you or lying at your feet. It is only taken when the build finishes, so a cancelled build costs you nothing. The Crafting window lists what a recipe still needs.

## Machine parts

Gear sets, shafts, bearings, springs and drive belts are items you make from a material of your choice. Examine a part to see how much work it does and how long it lasts compared with the standard one; steel is the standard for gears, shafts, bearings and springs, and leather for belts.

| Part | By hand | At an anvil, under **Parts** | Cast in a parts mold |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Gear set | Wood or bone (4), with a chisel | Bronze, iron or steel (4) | Copper, bronze, brass, iron or steel |
| Shaft | Wood (2), with a hatchet | Bronze, iron or steel (2) | Copper, bronze, brass, iron or steel |
| Bearing | Wood (2) and 1 animal fat, with a chisel | Bronze, iron or steel (2), as a bushing | Copper, bronze, brass, iron or steel |
| Spring | - | Bronze, iron or steel (2) | - |
| Drive belt | Rope (3), leather (2) or cloth (3), with a knife | Bronze, iron or steel (3), as a drive chain | - |

- **By hand:** hold the stack, open its crafting menu and look in **Machine parts**. The tool can be held, worn or at your feet.
- **At an anvil:** use bronze, iron or steel on the anvil and pick **Parts**.
- **Cast:** shape an **unfired parts mold** from 3 clay (in **Molds**, with Bronze Working), fire it like any other mold, fill it from a blacksmith pot and use it on an anvil. You pick the shape there. Brass now pours into every mold at the same cost as bronze.

Wooden parts are the cheapest and the weakest: a wooden gear set does about two fifths of the work of a steel one and wears out five times as fast. Bronze and brass come close to iron. Metals that are too soft, such as lead, tin and gold, cannot be made into gears, shafts or bearings at all. Carving parts by hand needs Basic Toolmaking; springs need Bronze Working.

## Lathes

A part you carve, forge or cast is **rough**. A lathe makes **precise** parts; examine a part to see which it is.

| Lathe | Research | Built from | Cuts | You also need |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pole lathe | Workshops | 8 wood and 2 rope | Wood, bone and brass | A chisel held, worn or at your feet |
| Engine lathe | Machined Tools | 12 iron, plus a gear set, a shaft and 2 bearings of any material, with a wrench | Copper, bronze, brass, iron and steel | Nothing; it works twice as fast |

Both are in the **Economy & production** crafting menu of their main material and are fastened down with a wrench like other workshop machines. The parts that go into an engine lathe can be rough, so you never need a lathe to build your first one.

- **Turn a new part:** use a stack of the material on the lathe and pick a gear set (4), shaft (2) or bearing (2).
- **True a rough part:** use a rough gear set, shaft or bearing on the lathe. It keeps its material and becomes precise.

Springs, drive belts and chains are never turned.

## Sparks

Iron, steel and flint throw sparks. If you strike a barrel that holds gunpowder or fuel with a tool made of one of them while on **Harm intent**, there is a small chance each blow sets it off. Tools of bronze, copper, wood and bone never spark, so keep a bronze or copper set for work around powder and fuel. Examine a barrel to see whether its contents are dangerous. Filling, pouring and carrying a barrel are always safe.
