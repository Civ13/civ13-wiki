# Guide to Pollution

Industry and spilled chemicals soil the ground around them. Polluted ground slows plant growth, and heavily polluted ground stops it completely, so keep smoky workshops away from your fields.

## What pollutes

- **Burning fuel.** Furnaces, bloomeries, blast furnaces and factory machines pollute the most, out to about three tiles. Kilns pollute less. Ovens, wood stoves, campfires and other cooking fires pollute only a little, right around themselves.
- **Steam boilers.** A lit heat source for a steam engine pollutes the ground around it for as long as it burns.
- **Engines.** Combustion engines pollute the ground around them while they run, more under heavy load. Solar panels and other clean power do not.
- **Spills.** Petroleum, gasoline, diesel, biodiesel, mercury and acids spilled on the ground soil the tile and its neighbours. Mercury is the worst.

Pollution is strongest at the source and fades with distance. A single smelt or meal does little; a furnace kept running all day fouls the ground around it.

## How to spot it

Polluted ground shows soot stains that get darker as the pollution builds up:

- **Light soot:** a warning. Plants still grow normally.
- **Grimy, darker stains:** plants grow at about half speed and wild plants no longer appear.
- **Black stains with oily patches:** nothing grows. Crops and tree seedlings stop where they are, chopped stumps do not grow back and no new trees spread onto the tile.

Examine the ground from up to two tiles away to see how polluted it is. Skilled farmers see the exact amount, out of 100. Examining a struggling crop also tells you when pollution is the reason.

Ploughing, digging or building a floor does **not** clean the ground; the pollution stays with the tile.

## Cleaning it up

- **Time.** Pollution fades by itself, slowly. Heavily polluted ground can take a few hours to recover once the source stops.
- **Rain** washes the ground and doubles the speed of recovery.
- **Grass and trees** help: grass recovers a little faster, and ground under a tree recovers three times as fast. Plant trees around the edge of an industrial area as the ground recovers.
- **Charcoal.** Hold charcoal and click a polluted tile to scatter it over the ground. One piece soaks up a good deal of pollution on that tile and some on the tiles around it. Ovens sometimes leave charcoal behind after cooking, and a campfire leaves some when it burns out.

## Tips

- Build furnaces, factories and engines at least four or five tiles from fields and orchards.
- Store and refuel with fuel carefully; a dropped bucket of gasoline next to a field ruins those crops.
- If a field has gone black, move the source first, then clean the field with charcoal, or let rain and time do it.
