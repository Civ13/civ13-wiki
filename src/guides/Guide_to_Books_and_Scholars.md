# Books and Scholars

On persistent worlds, knowledge is written down instead of handed out. Nobody can craft anything they haven't studied, research is written into books, and the world only leaves the Stone Age when someone discovers the way out. This guide covers studying, researching, writing and copying books, and the scholar's trade.

```admonish info
This applies to persistent worlds such as [Gaia](../maps/Gaia.md) and the regions connected to it. Round maps such as [Civilizations & Nomads](../gamemodes/Civilizations_and_Nomads.md) still use the faction [Research](Guide_to_Research.md) tree. The node names, prerequisites and prototypes in that guide's [node reference](Guide_to_Research.md#full-node-reference) are the same here.
```

## Getting started

The world starts in 5000 B.C. and has no clock: it moves into a new age only when someone finishes a **capstone** chapter (the prototype nodes, such as Bronze Working), and every player is told when that happens.

1. **Found a faction.** The founder receives the **Stone Age master book**, which holds every Stone Age chapter, and a **pencil**. Nobody knows those chapters yet.
2. **Study the chapters you need.** Hold the book, use it and press **Study** beside a chapter. Each chapter needs its prerequisites first, so work through the book in order.
3. **Craft what you've learned.** Crafting checks your own knowledge only. Your faction's research and the world's age don't unlock anything for you.
4. **Research new chapters** at a research bench, then study them like any other chapter.

**Pencils, paper and blank books** can be crafted by anyone from the start, with no knowledge needed. They are made from wooden planks, which anyone can cut from logs with an axe or by hand. For a new character's first steps, see [Your First Day](Guide_to_Your_First_Day.md). **Parchment** is cut from a dry hide with a sharp tool (four sheets per hide) and works like paper. In the Stone Age a sheet of paper is called **papyrus**; it is the same sheet. Sheets clipped together in a paper bundle can be used straight from the bundle. See [Parchment](../Miscellaneous_Crafting.md#parchment) for the whole hide route.

## Studying

Studying a chapter takes 1 minute in the Stone Age plus 30 seconds for each later age, and you keep it until you die. You can study from a book in hand, from a loose chapter page, or from a book lying on a research bench. Studying needs no scholarship, only the chapter's prerequisites.

You can stop studying at any time: walk away, put the book down or get interrupted, and the part you already read is kept. Next time, the chapter shows how far you got (for example **Continue: Iron Tools (40% read)**) and studying it only takes the time left. Your progress belongs to you, not the book, so you can continue from any copy of the chapter, after a restart or in another region. Recipe manuals work the same way.

Using a research book in hand opens its window. Alt-click does the same for a book lying next to you. Its chapters are sorted for you: first the ones you can study now (**Ready to study**), then the ones whose prerequisites you still need (**Needs groundwork**), then the ones you already know (**Known**). Each row shows the age, the discipline, how long studying takes and, when it is locked, which chapters to learn first. Type in **Find a chapter** to search by chapter, recipe or discipline, use the buttons under it to show one group, and click a row to see its groundwork and the recipes it teaches. Examining a book shows the same three groups. Recipe compendiums sort their chapters the same way.

**Death wipes everything you know**, both chapters and scholarship. A new character starts with nothing, so keep your books safe and your scholars alive.

## Research benches

A **research bench** costs 8 soft wood. Benches belong to nobody: anyone can use any bench, and a bench holds only the work in progress.

1. Put a book on the bench, click a subject in the bench window and press **Research this here**. The research is written into that book. Take the book away and the unfinished work goes with it.
2. Stay beside the bench. It only makes progress while someone within one tile knows every prerequisite of the subject and holds a writing tool (see [The scholar's trade](#the-scholars-trade)).
3. Speed it up by using **samples** on the bench: existing examples of things the subject unlocks. Capstones also need their **prototype** before the chapter is finished.
4. When the research is done, the chapter is written into the book. **It teaches nobody**, not even the researcher. Whoever wants it still has to study it.

The first time anyone finishes a capstone chapter, the whole world moves into the next age.

### Losing an age

An age lasts only as long as its capstone is written down somewhere. If every book, loose chapter page and recipe manual that holds the current age's capstone is destroyed, and no capstone of a later age survives either, the world falls back one age within about ten minutes, and every player is told. If the age before it has lost its capstone too, that one goes next, a little later. The Stone Age is never lost.

Nobody forgets anything when an age is lost. Anyone who still knows the lost capstone can **write it from memory** into a book, and the world returns to that age at once. A region connected to a mainland never falls below the mainland's age.

### The bench window

- **Books on the bench** shows both books and their chapters. A chapter you can study is marked, and clicking any chapter opens its details.
- **You have** lists what you are holding (writing tool and blank sheets) and your scholarship.
- **Currently researching** shows the progress bar, a checklist of what the work still needs (a book, a researcher beside the bench, light, an instrument), a warning when supplies have run out, and roughly how long is left at the current pace. **Stop researching** halts it; the unfinished work stays in the book.
- **Find technology** searches by subject, recipe or discipline. The buttons under it show only what is **Ready to study**, what you **Can research**, what is **In progress**, what is **Known** or what is **Not yet** open to you. Switch between **Tree** and **List** to see the subjects as rows or as the tree.
- Click a subject for its details: the groundwork it needs (click one to jump to it), what researching it needs and whether you meet each point, how long studying, researching and writing it take, what it leads to and the recipes it unlocks.
- The buttons in the details do the work in one click: **Study this chapter**, **Research this here**, **Add to queue**, **Copy into the other book** and **Write it from memory**. In the list view each row has its own **Study** or **Write** button.
- **Queue** up to 12 subjects. When one is finished the bench starts the next one that is ready.

## Late research

From era 5 on (the ages after the Industrial Age), research is more than waiting at a bench. Bench study alone still finishes any subject, and late subjects are studied at the bench 50% faster than their listed time, but the quickest way through is to go out and use what you are studying.

### Field work

Most late subjects list one or two pieces of **field work** in their details. Do the work anywhere, then stand at a bench that is researching the subject: your experience is written into the book as a share of the subject, usually between a fifth and a half. Each book takes each piece of field work once, and the bench window shows how far along you are.

| Field work | Counts towards |
| --- | --- |
| Firing firearms | Cartridge Firearms, Self-Loading Firearms, Automatic Weapons, Mechanized Warfare, Missiles, Modern Armor, Infantry Support Weapons, Guided Missiles |
| Flying an aircraft as its pilot | Aviation & Rocketry, Naval Aviation, Missiles, Rocketry, Crewed Flight, Pressure Suits, Drones |
| Completing surgery steps | Anesthetic Surgery, Modern Medicine, Emergency Medicine |
| Diagnosing sick patients with a doctor's handbook | Pharmacology, Germ Theory, Public Health, Emergency Medicine |
| Running syntheses at a reaction bench | Pharmacology, Synthetic Fabrics, Modern Medicine, Life Support, Local Resources |
| Analysing survey samples at a mission console | Planetary Survey and the space subjects after it |
| Harvesting crops | Soil Science, Crop Breeding |

Your experience stays with your character, but it does not come with you when you travel to another region.

### Knowing a subject

Some late subjects do more than unlock recipes. Once you have **studied** the chapter, you carry its benefit yourself; the details of each subject show it under **Knowing it**.

| Subject | What knowing it does |
| --- | --- |
| Alloys | Metal tools and weapons you forge last 30% longer. |
| Advanced Agriculture | Your harvests give 25% more crop and seed. |
| Anesthetic Surgery | Each surgery step you perform is 15% more likely to succeed. |
| Modern Medicine | You finish each surgery step a quarter faster. |
| Public Health | You are half as likely to catch an illness from people nearby or from poor hygiene. |
| Aviation & Rocketry | Aircraft you pilot burn a quarter less fuel in the air. |
| Soil Science | Your harvests leave the soil richer, so the same plot can be sown again sooner. |
| Crop Breeding | Your harvests give a further 25% more crop and seed. |

### Big science

Five of the largest subjects are meant for a team. Each wants a **second scholar** of another discipline standing at the bench, and a **facility** within three tiles of it.

| Subject | Second scholar | Facility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nuclear Power | Engineering | A reaction bench |
| Digital Computing | Engineering | A foundry workbench |
| Rocketry | Engineering | A foundry workbench |
| Orbital Flight | Natural Science | A launch rail |
| Modern Medicine | Natural Science | An operating table |

The second scholar needs half the level that leading a subject of that age in their discipline would take. A lone scholar can still finish: without the second scholar the work runs at a quarter speed, and without the facility at half speed. On every other late subject, a second scholar who could research it too makes the work 25% faster.

### Rushing

**Rush the work** in the bench window (**Work carefully** turns it off again) makes a late subject go 50% faster, with a small chance of an accident each time the bench makes progress. An accident undoes a little of the work and hurts: radiation for Nuclear Power, a blast for science and military subjects, poisoning for medicine and farming, a crushed hand for engineering, or just a mistake to throw out. The further your skill is above what the subject needs, the rarer accidents are. Careful work is the default and never has accidents.

### Later subjects

Health, Agriculture and Military carry on past the subjects listed in the [node reference](Guide_to_Research.md#full-node-reference):

| Subject | Era | Needs | Gives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Soil Science | 7 | Hydroponics | Richer soil after your harvests |
| Infantry Support Weapons | 7 | Field Artillery, Automatic Weapons | Foldable mortar, mortar shells and smoke shells (steel) |
| Emergency Medicine | 8 | Modern Medicine, Anesthetic Surgery | Defibrillator and power cells (electronics), autoinjectors and body bags (plastic) |
| Crop Breeding | 8 | Soil Science | Bigger harvests |
| Guided Missiles | 8 | Missiles, Infantry Support Weapons | Foldable anti-tank guided missile system and its HEAT and HE missiles (steel) |

A crafted autoinjector is empty: fill it from a container like a syringe. It injects its whole dose in a second and works once.

## Writing and copying books

Books are ordinary items. They can be traded, lent, hidden, stolen or carried to another region, and they also still work as journals.

| Action | How | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Write notes** | Use a pencil or pen on a book and choose **Contents** to add a page of your own writing. Use the book in hand to read it. | Instant |
| **Write from memory** | Put the book down next to you, hold a pencil or pen and Alt-click the book, then press **Write** beside a chapter you know. At a bench, press **Write it from memory** in the subject's details. A blank sheet of paper, papyrus or parchment is taken from your other hand, your bags or a paper bundle you carry (taking one off a bundle needs a free hand). | 2 minutes in the Stone Age, +1 minute per later age; costs stamina |
| **Copy at a bench** | Put two books on a bench, open a chapter and press **Copy into the other book**, or press the **Copy all ... chapters** button under a book to copy everything the other book lacks, one after another. Hold a pencil or pen and have blank sheets on you. You don't need to know the chapter. | Same as writing |
| **Rip out a page** | Hold the book, click the chapter in its window and press **Tear this chapter out**. A chapter becomes a loose page that can be studied on its own. | 2 seconds |
| **Bind a page back in** | Use the loose page on a book. | 30 seconds |
| **Print a book** | Put the book in a printing press as the original and a blank book as the copy, then use the press. It prints the pages, title and every finished chapter, using one sheet of printing paper per chapter. Load printing paper by using blank paper, papyrus, parchment, a paper bundle or a paper bin on the press. Unfinished research isn't printed. | 25 seconds |

### Ciphers

A scholar who knows **Printing Press** can lock a research book. Use a pen on the book, choose **Cipher** and set a key word. It takes 30 seconds.

- Nobody can study from an enciphered book, copy its chapters or tear pages out of it without the key.
- Tell the key word to anyone you trust. They use a pen on the book, choose **Cipher** and enter it once; after that the book stays open to them. The same menu lets a reader remove the cipher.
- The cipher is as strong as its writer's Basic Mathematics: strength 1 with no training, one more for every 20 levels, up to 6.
- A scholar with at least 12 levels of Basic Mathematics and a pen can **break** a cipher without the key, in 30 second sittings. A strength 1 cipher takes about two minutes for an equal; stronger ciphers and better writers take longer, up to three times as long, and a much better code-breaker takes as little as half. Progress is kept with the book, so you can come back to it.
- A printing press copies the cipher along with the book. An enciphered book still counts as a written record of its age.

Recipe manuals and recipe compendiums take written notes too: use the book and choose **Read the notes** or study as usual. Binding manuals together keeps their notes. Research chapters only go into ordinary books, and a manual with notes in it can't be carried to another region.

A bench holds up to two books. Research goes into the first one; **Write research into the other book** swaps them.

## Scholarship

Research trains six disciplines, from 0 to 100: **Basic Mathematics, Engineering, Natural Science, Medicine, Agriculture** and **Military Science**. The **Scholarship** tab in the side panel shows your level in each discipline, the latest age you can research in it, your title, your master and your apprentices; the bench shows what each chapter needs.

- Stone Age research needs no training. Each later age needs 12 more levels in the chapter's discipline, plus half that in Basic Mathematics. A Bronze Age chapter needs 12 and 6; an Imperial Age chapter needs 36 and 18.
- Skill above the requirement makes research faster, up to twice as fast.
- You gain skill by working at a bench, by studying chapters and by writing them. Work far below your level teaches you less, and half of what you learn also goes to Basic Mathematics.

## Practice

You can also train a discipline by hand. Hold its practice tool and use it in hand for a 45 second session. Each session costs some stamina, needs light and trains only that discipline. A writing desk beside you shortens the session, and your master standing nearby doubles what you learn.

| Discipline | Practice tool | How to make it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic Mathematics | Writing slate, or an **abacus** for 50% more | 2 stone (no knowledge needed); abacus 3 soft wood (Trade & Coinage) |
| Engineering | **Model kit**, or a drafting set for 50% more | 3 soft wood (Basic Toolmaking); drafting set 2 iron (Iron Tools) |
| Natural Science | **Specimen box** | 2 soft wood (Basic Toolmaking) |
| Medicine | **Study bones** | 2 bone (Basic Toolmaking) |
| Agriculture | **Seed tray** | 2 soft wood (Basic Toolmaking) |
| Military Science | **War game board** | 2 soft wood (Basic Toolmaking) |

Practice is a way to get started, not a way to the top. Each session teaches a little, less as you improve, and practice stops at level 24, enough to research two ages past a discipline's first and to earn the Apprentice title. Beyond that, only research, study and tutoring teach you more.

## The scholar's trade

| Need | Details |
| --- | --- |
| **Writing tool** | You must hold one to research. A **writing slate** (2 stone, no knowledge needed) or a pencil works at normal speed, a crayon at half speed, and a quill or pen 25% faster. Writing and copying need a pencil or pen, and a better pen writes faster. |
| **Light** | A bench makes no progress in the dark. Studying, writing and copying need light where you stand too: evening daylight is enough, but at night or in a dark room bring a candle, lamp or torch. If the light goes out you stop, and a chapter you were studying keeps the part you already read. |
| **Supplies** | Use supplies on a bench to stock it (up to 200 per discipline). Each bit of progress uses one. Without them, the bench works at half speed. |
| **Instruments** | From the Imperial Age on, a chapter needs its discipline's instrument on or next to the bench, or in a researcher's hands. Without it the bench makes no progress. |
| **Writing desk** | 6 soft wood (Basic Furniture). Studying, writing and copying beside one are 25% faster. |
| **Library** | Bookcases within 3 tiles of a bench. Each related chapter on the shelves (one in the subject's groundwork or its discipline) makes research 10% faster, up to 50%. |

| Discipline | Supplies | Instrument |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic Mathematics | Blank paper, papyrus or parchment, loose or in a paper bundle | **Abacus** (3 soft wood, Trade & Coinage) |
| Engineering | Material stacks (wood, stone, metal and so on) | **Drafting set** (2 iron, Iron Tools) |
| Natural Science | Ore or glass | Beaker or glass jar |
| Medicine | Medical supplies or grown herbs | Scalpel |
| Agriculture | Seeds or produce | Plough |
| Military Science | Ammunition or ammunition parts | Map |

## Teaching and apprentices

**Tutoring:** stand beside someone while they study a chapter you know. They finish in two thirds of the time, and if you're still there at the end they gain three times the usual skill and you gain some too. Selling books or tutoring is ordinary trade, so set your own price.

**Apprentices:** use **Take an apprentice** in the IC tab on a player next to you. They have to accept, and a master can have up to three. While their master is within 2 tiles, an apprentice gains twice the usual skill at a bench, and apprentices watching their master research within 2 tiles pick up a little skill too. The apprentice can end it with **Leave apprenticeship**, and it ends when the apprentice dies.

**Titles:** examining someone shows their best discipline as a title once it reaches 12: **Apprentice** from 12, **Scholar** from 40 and **Master** from 75, for example "Scholar of Medicine". Use it to find someone to hire.

## Tips

- Copy your important chapters. A raid on your library takes every chapter you only had once.
- Keep spare copies of each capstone in different places. If the last one burns, the whole world loses that age until someone who knows it writes it down again.
- A scribe doesn't need to be a scholar: anyone can copy chapters at a bench.
- New to a discipline? Practise with its tool between study sessions while your stamina recovers.
- Keep a bench lit, stocked and next to your bookcases, and research with a pen at a desk nearby.
- Rip out single chapters to lend or sell, and keep the master book at home.
