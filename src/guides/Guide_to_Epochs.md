# Epochs

Civ13 uses ten epochs. The date identifies the stage of the game, not the tier of a particular research bench; menus and research announcements sometimes use different names for the same date. These are game labels, not historical boundaries. Not every mode offers every starting epoch, and the list is not a live server status.

## How to use this guide

The advice below is for **persistent worlds**. Finishing a [capstone chapter](Guide_to_Books_and_Scholars.md#research-benches) moves the world into its next age. That opens the stage for new work; it does not teach every player its recipes. Study the relevant chapters and check your skills, stations and map's crafting catalog. Keep copies of important capstones in separate places so one loss does not erase the only written record. [Faction research](Guide_to_Research.md) works differently in other modes.

## 5000 B.C. — Stone Age

- **What changes:** This is the starting age. Flint tools, campfires and basic shelters get you through the first day. Founding a faction gives its founder a Stone Age master book and a writing slate; members still have to study the chapters.
- **Focus:** Get food, water and shelter sorted, then study **Basic Toolmaking** and set up a research bench. Start with [Your First Day](Guide_to_Your_First_Day.md).
- **Prepare for 313 B.C.:** Keep clay for the axehead mold prototype that completes **Bronze Working**. Locate copper and tin for bronze production once the chapter is learned.

## 313 B.C. — Bronze or Classical Age

- **What changes:** **Bronze Working** opens this age. Once studied, it supports bronze tools and other cast goods; the age also brings masonry and better containers through their own chapters.
- **Focus:** Get copper and tin reliably, fire clay molds, and set up a kiln so you can make bronze tools rather than replacing stone ones one at a time. See [Metallurgy](Guide_to_Metallurgy.md).
- **Prepare for 1013:** Have a scholar study **Bronze Working** and research **Iron Smithing**. Save clay for an ingot mold prototype, and locate iron and fuel for the next workshop.

## 1013 — Medieval Age

- **What changes:** **Iron Smithing** opens this age. Ironworking, brickmaking, glassworking and papermaking give settlements new ways to build, equip and teach.
- **Focus:** Establish a bloomery and learn **Iron Tools** so the settlement can make better equipment. Use paper and books to spread the knowledge beyond one smith or scholar.
- **Prepare for 1713:** Choose a route: **Papermaking** leads to **Printing Press** and its carved stamp prototype; **Iron Smithing** leads to **Gunpowder** and its own prototype. **Resource Processing** is also useful groundwork for Watermills.

## 1713 — Imperial Age

- **What changes:** **Printing Press** or **Gunpowder** opens this age. The press is a peaceful route into the same epoch; war-related recipes still need their own research.
- **Focus:** Spread useful chapters with [paperwork and printing](Guide_to_Paperwork.md), and develop water-powered workshops and bulk metal supply. **Watermills** and **Iron Smithing** are both needed for Steam Power.
- **Prepare for 1873:** Stock coal, iron ingots and copper ingots for the **Steam Power** bill. On persistent worlds you also need a recorded demonstration of a steam engine running for one minute; study alone does not finish it.

## 1873 — Industrial Age

- **What changes:** **Steam Power** opens the Industrial Age. Steam machinery, factories and rail make bulk production and freight practical as their own chapters are learned.
- **Focus:** Pick one production chain to keep supplied and use [rail](Guide_to_Trains.md) where hauling is the bottleneck. Grow your fuel and metal supply before building more machines.
- **Prepare for 1903:** Study **Galvanic Cells** and work toward **Electricity**. Get copper, zinc, lead and rubber for its bill, and demonstrate a lamp running on electricity for one minute.

## 1903 — Early Modern Age

- **What changes:** **Electricity** opens this age, called **WW1** in some research announcements. Power generation, batteries and wiring support machines; combustion and assembly lines open through separate chapters.
- **Focus:** Build a small, reliable [power grid](Guide_to_Power.md) and organize [factory production](Guide_to_Factory_Automation.md). Work out what your settlement can produce steadily and what you need to trade for.
- **Prepare for 1943:** Choose **Powered Flight** or **Mechanized Warfare**. Both need steel and rubber; the flight bill also needs brass and cloth, while the mechanized bill needs lead, sulphur and saltpeter. Read the full bill in the project book before hauling a shipment. On persistent worlds these two bills take the place of the prototype, but study is still required.

## 1943 — World War II

- **What changes:** **Powered Flight** or **Mechanized Warfare** opens this age. Aircraft and heavier industry have their own downstream work; the two capstones do not automatically grant each other's knowledge.
- **Focus:** Secure a supply of steel, rubber, fuel and spare parts before relying on [aircraft](Guide_to_Aircraft.md) or industrial machinery. Keep experienced crews and working infrastructure supplied.
- **Prepare for 1969:** **Nuclear Power** needs **Electricity** and **Alloys**, a recorded reactor demonstration (the bench asks for three minutes of safe power output), and a large bill of uranium, lead, steel and concrete.

## 1969 — Cold War

- **What changes:** **Nuclear Power** opens this age. With additional research, settlements can pursue automation and [electronic computing](Guide_to_Computers.md); the Moon also becomes eligible for the spaceflight research path.
- **Focus:** Learn to run and maintain your power and production systems before expanding them. Follow the [space station guide](Guide_to_Space_Stations.md) if you pursue spaceflight, and check the relevant region and research requirements.
- **Prepare for 2013:** Finish **Automation**, **Electricity** and **Electronic Computing** for **Digital Computing**. Plan to run a computerized fabricator for one minute and deliver the bill of copper, aluminium, gold, silver, electronics and gems.

## 2013 — Modern Age

- **What changes:** **Digital Computing** opens this age. It gives settlements a foundation for more advanced electronics and standard reactor equipment; the age itself is not a shortcut to Digital Age networks or consumer devices.
- **Focus:** Make electronics production reliable. Develop and teach enough specialists to keep power and computing working without relying on one person.
- **Prepare for 2026:** **Digital Networks** builds on **Digital Computing**. It needs a connected mobile call lasting one minute and a larger bill of copper, aluminium, gold, silver, electronics and gems. Arrange suppliers early and keep spare copies of the chapters.

## 2026 — Digital Age

- **What changes:** **Digital Networks** opens the last listed age. Further research includes **Consumer Electronics**, **3D Printing**, **Smart Home** and **Drones**. Mars becomes eligible for the applicable settlement research and travel path.
- **Focus:** Pick useful projects and maintain the electricity, parts and material supply they depend on. Keep teaching and copying newer research that other players do not already know.
- **Prepare for what comes after:** There is no later epoch. Plan for ongoing maintenance and, if your group pursues off-world settlement, secure life support, replacement equipment and a route home before committing to a base.
