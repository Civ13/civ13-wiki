# Rivers & Floods

On lands with hills, such as [Gaia](../maps/Gaia.md), rivers rise and fall. A long rain or a spring thaw can push a river over its banks and onto the low ground beside it. This page covers when rivers rise, what floodwater does to you, and how to keep your home out of it.

## When rivers rise

- **Rain.** Storms raise every river whose land they rain on. A storm upstream sends a rise downstream a few minutes later, so a river can climb after the rain has stopped where you are.
- **Snowmelt.** Snow that falls in winter stays on the hills and runs into the rivers in spring. Spring brings the biggest floods.
- **Seasons.** Rivers run high in spring and in the wet season. They run low at the end of summer, in the dry season and under a heat wave.
- **Size.** A big river with a lot of land draining into it rises highest and stays up longest. A small stream rises a little and drops quickly.

Floods come only from rivers, lakes and swamps. The sea does not flood the land.

A flood fills land in order of height. Banks a step above the water go under first. Land behind a ridge stays dry until the water tops the ridge. Water you see standing on flat ground near a river during a storm is a warning that the river is already high.

## Walking in floodwater

| Water | What it does to you |
| --- | --- |
| Ankle-deep | A little slower, and your feet get wet. |
| Knee-deep | Slower again. |
| Waist-deep | As slow as a flooded deck. |
| Over your head | You must swim. You tire and drown just as in deep water. |

Boots made for wading help in shallow water, as they do in a river's shallows. A boat, a lifejacket or a diving suit keeps you up in deep floodwater. Use **Check Movement** (IC tab) to see how deep the floodwater under you is and how much it slows you.

## Fire

Floodwater puts out fires on the ground, and it puts out campfires that it reaches. A campfire under water can't be lit until the water goes down, so build your fire on high ground.

## Rivers in flood

- **Faster current.** A high river carries swimmers, empty rafts and anything left in the water twice as far. At low water the current is weak.
- **Waterwheels** make up to twice their power when the river is high and as little as half when it is low. Examine a waterwheel to see whether the river is high or low.

## Keeping dry

- **Build high.** The banks just above a river are the first land to flood. A step or two of height keeps a home dry through most floods.
- **Raise the ground.** Put a pile of dirt on a tile and pack it with a stone rammer to raise it one level (see [Gaia](../maps/Gaia.md)). Raised ground stays dry until the flood rises above its new height.
