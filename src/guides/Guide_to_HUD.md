# Customizing Your HUD

You can resize the HUD (the buttons, meters and inventory slots around the
edge of the screen), move tiles to new places, and lock them in place once
you are happy with them. All three options are verbs in the **OOC** tab.

## HUD Scale

Use **HUD Scale** to make the whole HUD bigger or smaller. You can pick from
50% up to 200%. Larger scales are easier to read on big or high-resolution
screens. Each tile stays on the edge or corner of the screen it sits on, so
the HUD stays out of the middle of your view at any scale.

Above 100% the standard HUD no longer fits in one row, so it rearranges
itself: the status column on the right stacks up the right edge, and the
bottom bar wraps into extra rows. No tile covers another. Tiles you have
moved or resized yourself stay where you put them.

## Moving, resizing and locking tiles

1. Use **Edit HUD Layout**. While you are editing, unlocked tiles turn
   green and locked tiles turn red.
2. **Drag** a tile to move it. Anything you are wearing in an inventory
   slot moves along with its slot. The action buttons along the top move
   together as one bar, and the vehicle controls can be moved while you are
   driving.
3. **Scroll the mouse wheel** over a tile to make that one tile bigger or
   smaller, from half size up to three times its size.
4. **Click** a tile to lock or unlock it. A locked tile cannot be dragged or
   resized until you unlock it again.
5. Use **Edit HUD Layout** again when you are done. Your layout is saved and
   the HUD goes back to working normally.

To move a bag's storage window, open the bag before you start editing, then
drag the window by its slots, its close button or anything inside it. It can
be locked like a tile, but not resized. At HUD scales above 100% the storage
window opens above the HUD instead of on top of it.

While you are editing, clicking a tile does not use it. For example,
clicking a hand slot will not swap hands. Tiles you place near an edge or
corner of the screen stay there if your game window changes size.

## Resetting

**Reset HUD Layout** offers two choices:

- **Unlocked tiles** puts every unlocked tile back where it started, and
  leaves your locked tiles alone.
- **All tiles and the HUD scale** brings back the standard layout at 100%.

## Widescreen view

The game view is 21 tiles wide and 15 tall, so the map fills more of a wide
monitor and the chat and status panel take less room. Your inventory stays in
the bottom-left corner, your hands sit in the middle of the bottom edge, and
the combat buttons and status column follow the right edge.

On a small or square monitor, turn on **Square game view (small monitors)** in
your preferences to go back to a 15 by 15 view. The HUD and the side panel
adjust as soon as you change it.

## Fitting the game window

If black bars appear beside the map after you resize the window, choose
**Icons > Fit viewport (remove black bars)** from the menu bar, or use the
**Fit Viewport** verb in the OOC tab. The map pane snaps to the width of the
view and the chat and status panel take the rest.

The game also refits on its own whenever you resize or maximize the window,
and when you pick a size from the **Icons** menu. To keep the width you set
by dragging the divider, turn off **Auto-adjust game screen** in your
preferences. Dragging the divider never triggers a refit by itself.

## Hover names

Point the mouse at anything you can see, such as a tree, an animal, a person
or an item in your hands or an open bag, and its name appears in a small
panel just below the cursor. If the text is too big or too small, use
**Hover Text Size** in the **OOC** tab and pick from 50% to 200%. It is
separate from **HUD Scale**, so you can keep a large HUD with small hover
text, or the other way around. The panel follows the mouse, moves above the
cursor near the bottom of the screen and is always drawn on top of the HUD.
People wearing masks show the name you can actually see. Trees deep in a
forest show their names too, as long as you can see part of their canopy.

To hide the panel completely, use **Toggle Hover Text** in the **OOC** tab.
Use it again to bring the panel back. The game remembers your choice.

## Good to know

- Your layout and scale are saved with your player preferences, so they
  follow you between rounds and servers.
- Each HUD style keeps its own layout. If you change styles with
  **ChangeHUD**, you get that style's layout, and switching back restores
  the one you made before.
