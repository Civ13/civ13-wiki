# Gaia

Gaia is the authored Nomads map named **Nomads (Gaia)**. Its surface is **z=2**; **z=1** is the underground layer.

Gaia is one of the persistent lands in the same group as [North and South America](The_Americas.md). It is not in the round map vote. New characters start in North America, not here.

## Getting there

Gaia lies across the sea to the east of the Americas. Build a ship, bring it near the east edge of North or South America and set out on a voyage: Gaia is on the list, about 6 minutes' sailing from North America and 7 from South America (see [Sailing between the continents](../guides/Guide_to_Boats.md#sailing-between-the-continents)). You arrive anchored off Gaia's west coast, and sail back the same way. A plane can fly here too (see [Flying to another continent](../guides/Guide_to_Aircraft.md#flying-to-another-continent)). A trail marked out by players can also lead here once the staff approve it (see [Marking out a new trail](../guides/Guide_to_Travel.md#marking-out-a-new-trail)).

## What Gaia has that the Americas do not

- **An underground level** (z=1) below the surface, with rock to mine.
- **Taller buildings.** A roof can carry up to four floors above the ground.
- **A seabed under the deep ocean**, so [submarines](../guides/Guide_to_Boats.md#submarines) can dive, and [aircraft carriers](../guides/Guide_to_Aircraft_Carriers.md) and freighters with a hold below the waterline can be built.

## Hills

Gaia's surface has hills. Soft lines on the ground follow each height level: where the lines sit close together the slope is steep, and where there are none the ground is flat. Light comes from the north-west, so lines and slopes facing north-west are a warm, lighter colour and those facing south-east are darker. Water and beaches are not shaded.

The land follows the map. Beaches and marshes are low and flat, and the coastal plain behind them rolls into gentle hills. The rocky ranges are high plateaus: where a range stands close to the sea its sides are steep, a hard climb of a few tiles, while from inland and through the passes at the ends of a range the climb is long and gentle. Rocky shores can be cliffs, rivers run through gorges with one high bank, the desert's lakes sit in craters with steep rims, and long ridges cross the larger continents with a steep face on one side and an easy slope on the other.

Walking speed on slopes, tripping, sight lines, bullet drop, tamping paths, grading hills and the **Check Movement** verb work the same way here as on the Americas: see [Hills](The_Americas.md#hills).

## Rivers and floods

Gaia's rivers rise after long rain and in the spring thaw, and spill onto the low ground beside them, as on the Americas. Cellars and mines near a rising river seep water. See [Rivers & Floods](../guides/Guide_to_Floods.md).

## Weather

Gaia has the same seasons as other Nomads maps, but clear spells last longer: while the sky is clear, rain, snow and storms start only about half as often. Once bad weather has started it runs its usual course.
