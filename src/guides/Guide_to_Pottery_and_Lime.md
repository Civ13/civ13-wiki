# Pottery, Bricks & Lime

Six workshop stations make clay, glass, stone and furnace work faster. Everything they make can still be made by hand; the stations save time, clay or fuel.

Fasten or unfasten a station with a wrench. To take a wheel, bench, bellows or saw apart, hit it with a hammer on **Harm intent**.

## Potter's wheel

Use **clay** on the wheel and choose what to throw: pots, cups, bowls, jugs, pitchers, vases, cooking pots, crocks and vessels of your own design (see **[Making a Home](Guide_to_Homes.md#pottery-of-your-own-design)**). You only see the wares you already know how to make.

Each piece costs the same clay as shaping it by hand but takes a third of the time. The wheel keeps turning out the same ware until your clay runs out or you step away. The pieces come off unfired, so put them in a fire as usual to finish them.

## Brick mould bench

Use **4 clay** on the bench and choose a brick. Ten seconds later you have **eight** unfired bricks, and the bench keeps going while you have clay.

- **Clay blocks** and **bricks** cost the same clay as by hand, but come eight at a time.
- **Cement bricks** need <img src="../images/Quicklime.png"> **quicklime**. Use quicklime on the bench to fill its tub (it holds 20). Each batch of eight cement bricks then takes 4 clay and 2 quicklime. By hand, a single cement brick takes 4 clay.
- **By hand**, clay bricks and cement bricks are mixed wet: each one also takes 10 units of water from a bucket or bowl you hold or stand on. It's free while you work beside running water: a river with a current, an aqueduct, a fountain, a tap or a sink. Paper sheets take water the same way.

Fire the bricks to finish them.

## Bellows

Build the bellows on a tile next to a bloomery, blast furnace, kiln or lime kiln. Click them with an **empty hand** to pump for three seconds.

For the next minute and a half, a firing you start in a furnace beside the bellows takes a quarter less time and a quarter less fuel. Pump first, then light the furnace. One set of bellows blows on every furnace next to it.

## Lime kiln

1. Use **stone** or **marble** on the kiln. It holds 20 blocks.
2. Add fuel: wood, planks, charcoal or coal.
3. Click the kiln with an empty hand to light it.

After about 40 seconds it leaves one quicklime for each block. Right-click the kiln and choose **Empty** to take an unburnt charge back out.

Quicklime goes into cement bricks at the brick mould bench, and into concrete at a [cement mixer](Guide_to_Construction.md#concrete).

## Glassblowing bench

Build the bench on a tile next to a bloomery, blast furnace or kiln that has fuel in it. The fire does not need to be lit for a smelt; the bench reheats the glass in it.

Use **glass** on the bench and choose what to blow: drinking glasses, mugs, teacups and teapots, bottles, jars, vials, beakers, droppers and lightbulbs. Each go costs the same glass and time as making one piece by hand, but you get **two** pieces. It also burns a little fuel from the fire, so keep the furnace stocked. Reinforced glass cannot be blown.

## Mason's saw

Use **stone**, **marble** or **sandstone** blocks on the saw. Every two seconds it cuts:

- one stone block into two stone bricks,
- one marble block into two marble bricks,
- three sandstone blocks into two stone bricks.

By hand each of those cuts gives a single brick. The saw keeps working through the stack in your hand until it runs out or you step away. You need to know **Brickmaking** to cut bricks, the same as by hand.

## Mechanical power

Four prime movers turn the workshop machines standing next to them, so each step of the work takes less time. A mover drives every fastened **potter's wheel**, **mason's saw**, **cement mixer**, **shear legs** and **treadwheel crane** within one tile of it. You still work the machine yourself; it just goes faster.

Each mover is built from wood plus [machine parts](Guide_to_Tool_Materials.md) of any material, held or at your feet.

| | Mover | Made from | Research | Turns when | Work takes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <img src="../images/Treadwheel.png"> | **Treadwheel** | 12 wood, 1 shaft, 2 bearings | Stone Masonry | someone stands inside the wheel | 65% of the time |
| <img src="../images/Horse_gin.png"> | **Horse gin** | 10 wood, 1 gear set, 1 shaft, 1 bearing | Resource Processing | a living horse or head of cattle stands next to it | 50% |
| <img src="../images/Mill_wheel.png"> | **Mill wheel** | 14 wood, 1 gear set, 1 shaft, 2 bearings | Watermills | it stands next to flowing river water | 40% |
| <img src="../images/Windmill.png"> | **Windmill** | 16 wood, 1 gear set, 1 shaft, 2 bearings | Watermills | it stands under open sky and the wind blows | 70% in a light wind, 50% in a moderate one, 40% in a strong wind or gale |

- A mover must be fastened (use a wrench), and so must the machine it drives.
- Click a mover with an empty hand, or examine it, to see whether it is turning and why not.
- The treadwheel needs a second person: one treads inside the wheel while the other works the machine.
- Still water and ice do not turn a mill wheel, and a windmill stands idle in a calm.
- If two movers reach the same machine, the faster one counts.

Hit a mover with a hammer on **Harm intent** to take it apart. Movers are too heavy to pull up a ladder.

The same movers drive a [trip hammer](Guide_to_Metallurgy.md#trip-hammer) beside an anvil.
